Sources & how we verify

Accuracy over completeness. A fee, rule or form ships as “verified” only when at least two independent published sources agree on it. Official sources come first — the Indian Succession Act, 1925 itself, State Court Fees Acts, and state portals. Where sources disagree, the item is shown as unresolved or left out entirely, with the reason written down here.

Known conflicts

Court fee — Karnataka

One guide says 2% with slabs capped ~₹50,000–1,00,000; two others say 'usually about 3%' with a cap of ₹1,00,000. No Karnataka Court Fees Act schedule text was found in the sources checked.

No single figure is published for Karnataka — check the District Court or the State Court Fees Act schedule.

Court fee — Tamil Nadu

LawTendo gives two different tables in one article (fixed ₹100/250/500 then 3% above ₹50,000; and a separate 2%/3%/4%/5% slab table). RightToInformation.wiki says 3% capped at ₹3,00,000. SupremeToday identifies the provision (Sch.I Art.7, TN Court Fees and Suits Valuation Act 1955) but gives no figures.

No slab table is published for Tamil Nadu — check the District Court or the Act schedule.

Court fee — Delhi cap

One guide claims a ₹3,00,000 cap, but the Court Fees Act (Delhi Amendment) Sch.I Art.12 text found contains no cap.

The estimator uses 2.5% with no cap.

Uncontested timeline

Guides say 3–6 months (several), 4–7 months (Assetly), 5–7 months in West Bengal, and 6–12 months (EzyLegal NRI guide).

The site publishes the range 'commonly 3–7 months' with the caveat that court workload and objections extend it.

Legal-heir certificate timeline — Kerala

Most guides say 15–30 days; IndiaFilings/Housing.com describe a 30-day objection period plus 15 days to issue (about 45 days in total).

Both figures are shown on the Kerala page.

Legal-heir certificate timeline — Telangana

15 working days under the Telangana Right to Services Act per one guide; 7–14 working days per another.

No timeline is shown for Telangana.

What we deliberately do not cover

  • Probate of a will — the procedure depends on whether the will was registered, where the property sits, and local court practice. We do not publish a probate draft. Note: Section 213 (which once made probate compulsory in some cases) was omitted by the Repealing and Amending Act, 2025 (Act 37 of 2025; Presidential assent 20 December 2025, Gazette 21 December 2025). Probate was not abolished — it remains available voluntarily, and banks and other institutions may still ask for it. The wizard tells you when probate is the route and why.
  • Property title transfer — a succession certificate does not transfer title to immovable property. Mutation and registration are separate processes with their own state rules.
  • FormSutra's legal-heir guide — FormSutra already publishes a free legal-heir certificate guide with a document checklist. We do not duplicate it; our legal-heir pages add the per-state portal, fee and timeline that their single generic page does not have. Our succession-petition generator, decision wizard and court-fee estimator have no equivalent there.
