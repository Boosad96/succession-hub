Shares & Demat After Death — Transmission, Nominee & SEBI Value Tiers

Shares and Demat holdings pass by transmission after death — not by transfer. Whether you need a succession certificate depends on whether there is a nominee and on the value involved.

If there is a nominee

With a nominee, the nominee receives the securities as a trustee on behalf of the legal heir(s) — no succession certificate is needed. The documents are the transmission request form (TRF), the death certificate, the client master report and KYC. The nominee is a trustee, not the owner: the value belongs to the legal heirs under succession law.

If there is a joint holder

On the death of one joint holder, the surviving holder takes the securities by survivorship on the death certificate alone.

If there is no nominee — SEBI value tiers (Demat)

Up to ₹30,000: Quick Transmission Processing — a plain-paper request plus proof of relationship (immediate relatives only). Up to ₹30 lakh: simplified procedure — a notarised indemnity bond plus an affidavit-cum-NOC from all the heirs, or a family settlement deed. Above ₹30 lakh: an affidavit-cum-NOC plus one of — a will (with indemnity), a legal-heirship certificate (Tahsildar rank or above, with indemnity), or a succession certificate / letter of administration / court decree. For physical shares the tiers are ₹10,000 and ₹10 lakh. Probate of a will is no longer a mandatory requirement.

How fast must transmission happen

Entities should ordinarily complete transmission within 21 calendar days of receiving all the documents, giving written reasons for any delay or rejection.

Which certificate do you need for this?

No nominee + Demat holdings above ₹30 lakh: a succession certificate is one of three documents the depository may accept (the others are a will with indemnity, or a legal-heirship certificate with indemnity). Below ₹30 lakh, the simplified procedure applies and no succession certificate is needed. With a nominee, none is needed at any value.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a succession certificate for shares if there is a nominee?
No. With a nominee, the securities are transmitted to the nominee as a trustee for the legal heirs, on the transmission request form, the death certificate, the client master report and KYC. No succession certificate is needed.
What are SEBI's value tiers for transmission without a nominee?
For Demat holdings: up to ₹30,000 is Quick Transmission Processing; up to ₹30 lakh needs a notarised indemnity bond plus an affidavit-cum-NOC from all heirs (or a family settlement deed); above ₹30 lakh needs an affidavit-cum-NOC plus one of a will, a legal-heirship certificate, or a succession certificate / letter of administration / court decree. Physical shares use ₹10,000 and ₹10 lakh tiers.
Is the nominee the owner of the shares?
No. The nominee receives the securities as a trustee on behalf of the legal heir(s). The value belongs to the heirs under succession law.
How long does transmission take?
Ordinarily within 21 calendar days of receiving all the documents, with written reasons for any delay or rejection.
