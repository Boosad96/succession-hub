Insurance Claim After Death — Nominee vs Legal Heir

The person named as nominee on a life insurance policy is not necessarily the person who keeps the money. Under Indian law, the nominee is usually only a trustee for the legal heirs.

The nominee is a trustee, not the owner

The Supreme Court held in Sarbati Devi v. Usha Devi (1984) 1 SCC 424 that nomination under Section 39 of the Insurance Act, 1938 does not confer a beneficial interest: the nominee is only authorised to receive the amount, which forms part of the estate for distribution under succession law. This was reaffirmed in later cases, including Shakti Yezdani v. Jayanand Salgaonkar (2023). So the insurer pays the nominee, but the nominee holds the money in trust for the legal heirs.

The 'beneficial nominee' exception — unsettled law

The Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015 introduced Section 39(7): where the nominee is the policyholder's spouse, parent or child, the nominee is a 'beneficial nominee' entitled to retain the proceeds as beneficial owner (unless it is shown the policyholder could not validly confer that). Nominees who are anyone else hold as trustees. BUT High Courts have diverged on this (for example Allahabad High Court, Kusum v. Anand Kumar, 2025) and the Supreme Court has not conclusively settled it. We flag this as unsettled — do not treat it as a firm rule.

What the family should do

Claim from the insurer with the death certificate and the policy details. If there is a dispute between the nominee and the other legal heirs about who keeps the money, that dispute is decided under succession law — and a succession certificate is the document that authorises the heirs to collect it.

Which certificate do you need for this?

If the insurer pays the nominee, the nominee holds the money in trust for the legal heirs. If the heirs need to collect the amount themselves — for example where there is no nominee, or the nominee has also died — a succession certificate from the District Court is the usual route.

Frequently asked questions

Is the insurance nominee the owner of the money?
Usually no. The Supreme Court (Sarbati Devi v. Usha Devi, 1984) held that the nominee is only authorised to receive the amount; it forms part of the estate and belongs to the legal heirs under succession law.
What is a 'beneficial nominee' under Section 39(7)?
Since the 2015 amendment, a nominee who is the policyholder's spouse, parent or child is a 'beneficial nominee' entitled to retain the proceeds. But High Courts have diverged and the Supreme Court has not conclusively settled it — treat it as unsettled law, not a firm rule.
There is no nominee on the policy — what then?
The legal heirs claim from the insurer, and the insurer will usually ask for a succession certificate (or a legal-heir certificate) before paying.
