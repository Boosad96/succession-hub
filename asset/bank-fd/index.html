Bank FD & Deposits After Death — Nominee, ₹15 Lakh Threshold & Succession Certificate

Bank deposits are the most common reason families need a succession certificate — but since the RBI's 2025 Directions, many bank claims do not need one at all. It depends on whether there is a nominee and how much is involved.

If there is a nominee — no succession certificate, whatever the amount

Under the RBI (Settlement of Claims in respect of Deceased Customers of Banks) Directions, 2025 (26 September 2025), where there is a nominee or a survivorship clause, the bank must NOT insist on a succession certificate, probate, letters of administration, an indemnity bond or a surety — whatever the amount. The nominee needs only the claim form, the death certificate and ID/address proof. The nominee receives the money as a trustee for the legal heirs, not as the owner.

If there is no nominee — below the threshold (₹15 lakh / ₹5 lakh)

The threshold limit is ₹15 lakh for commercial and other banks and ₹5 lakh for co-operative banks (a bank may fix a higher limit). Below the threshold, the simplified procedure applies and no succession certificate is needed: claim form, death certificate, ID/address proof of the claimants, an indemnity bond (no third-party surety may be insisted on within the threshold), a no-objection from the non-claiming heirs, and proof of heirship via a legal-heir certificate or a declaration by an independent person known to the family. Conditions: no will, no contesting claim, and no court order restraining payment.

If there is no nominee — above the threshold

Above the threshold, the bank may settle on a succession certificate, OR a legal-heir certificate, OR a sworn affidavit (before a magistrate / judicial authority) — and it may seek a surety. Older guides say a succession certificate is 'the normal requirement' above the threshold; the 2025 Directions list it as one of three options, so do not treat it as mandatory — ask the bank which of the three it accepts.

How fast must the bank settle

The bank must settle a deposit claim within 15 calendar days of receiving all the required documents. Delay attracts interest of at least the Bank Rate + 4% per year for the delay period. The Directions were to be implemented by banks no later than 31 March 2026 — if your bank is still following an older process, point it at the 2025 Directions.

Which certificate do you need for this?

No will + no nominee + above ₹15 lakh: a succession certificate is the most widely accepted of the three options the bank may accept. Below the threshold, or with a nominee, you usually do not need one at all. Use the wizard to check your situation, and the petition draft if a succession certificate is the route.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a succession certificate if there is a nominee?
No. Under the RBI Directions, 2025, where there is a nominee or a survivorship clause, the bank must not insist on a succession certificate, probate, letters of administration, an indemnity bond or a surety — whatever the amount. The nominee only needs the claim form, the death certificate and ID/address proof.
What is the ₹15 lakh threshold?
Below ₹15 lakh (commercial and other banks) or ₹5 lakh (co-operative banks), and with no nominee, the bank uses a simplified procedure — claim form, death certificate, ID/address proof, an indemnity bond and an NOC from the non-claiming heirs. No succession certificate is needed below the threshold.
The bank is asking for a succession certificate above ₹15 lakh — is it mandatory?
Not under the 2025 Directions: above the threshold the bank may settle on a succession certificate, OR a legal-heir certificate, OR a sworn affidavit before a magistrate / judicial authority. A succession certificate is one of three options — ask the bank which it accepts.
How long should the bank take?
15 calendar days from receiving all the required documents. Delay attracts interest of at least the Bank Rate + 4% per year for the delay period.
