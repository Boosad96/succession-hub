Property After Death — Why a Succession Certificate Does Not Transfer Title

The most common misunderstanding in this whole area: a succession certificate does not transfer a house or a plot of land. It covers debts and securities only.

A succession certificate does not confer title

The Supreme Court held in Madhvi Amma Bhawani Amma v. Kunjikutty Pillai Meenakshi Pillai (2000) 6 SCC 301: 'The grant of a succession certificate does not confer any title to the property of the deceased. It merely authorizes the holder to collect the debts and securities.' It does not move a flat, a plot or a house into your name.

How property actually passes

Immovable property passes under personal law (or under the will, if there is one), and the heirs' names are then entered in the property records by mutation. A legal-heir certificate is likewise not proof of ownership — it identifies the heirs; it does not prove title.

Family settlement deed

If all the heirs agree on how the property is divided, a family settlement deed can record it without a court case. It must be voluntary, signed by all the members, with witnesses. If it creates, declares or extinguishes rights in immovable property in the present, it must be compulsorily registered under the Registration Act, 1908 (a mere memorandum recording a prior oral settlement need not be). Supreme Court: Kale v. Deputy Director of Consolidation.

Which certificate do you need for this?

For immovable property, the route is: establish who the heirs are (legal-heir certificate where issued, or probate if there is a will), then apply for mutation of the property records. A succession certificate is the wrong document for property — use the wizard to check, and do not pay a court fee for a certificate that cannot transfer title.

Frequently asked questions

Can I transfer a house with a succession certificate?
No. A succession certificate covers debts and securities only. The Supreme Court (Madhvi Amma, 2000) held it 'does not confer any title to the property of the deceased. It merely authorizes the holder to collect the debts and securities.'
How does property pass to the heirs then?
Under personal law (or the will, if there is one), and the heirs' names are entered in the property records by mutation. A legal-heir certificate identifies the heirs but is not proof of ownership.
All heirs agree on the division — do we need a court?
Not necessarily: a family settlement deed can record the division without a court case, signed by all the heirs with witnesses. But if it deals with immovable property rights, it must be registered under the Registration Act, 1908.
