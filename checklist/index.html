Timeline checklist — getting a succession certificate

The process, in order, with the time each step usually takes. Times are from published guides and vary by court and district — treat them as planning figures, not promises.

  1. Obtain the death certificate — The first document — every later step needs it: the petition, the bank, the EPF claim, the insurance claim and the legal-heir application.
    Sources: RegisterKaro — Succession certificate, InvestorHelpdesk — Legal heir certificate guide
  2. Identify the assets and all the heirs — List every debt and security (for a succession certificate) and every legal heir — leaving one out can cause rejection. Check which assets have a nominee, because a nominee changes the route.
    Sources: WillJini — Succession certificate for an NRI, eGovtSchemes — Varisu certificate (REV-114), Tamil Nadu
  3. Decide which certificate you need — No will + need to collect debts/securities → succession certificate (court). Only need to prove who the heirs are (pension, mutation, small claims) → legal-heir certificate (revenue). Will exists → probate / letters of administration (voluntary since Dec 2025). All heirs agree on the division → a family settlement deed may avoid court.
    Sources: Assetly — How to get a succession certificate in India, LegalServiceIndia — Legal heir certificate: law, procedure, documents, Finnovate — Probate in India explained (after Dec 2025)
  4. File the petition and pay the court fee — Petition under Section 372 before the District Judge where the deceased ordinarily resided (or where assets are). Pay the court fee on the schedule of debts/securities, via judicial stamp paper / deposit under Section 379.
    Sources: SooperKanoon — Indian Succession Act, 1925 Part X (bare text), RegisterKaro — Succession certificate
  5. Court notice and newspaper publication — The court issues notice to the heirs and publishes it in a newspaper (and in most states the official gazette) inviting objections. (Usually weeks 2–4 after filing)
    Sources: Assetly — How to get a succession certificate in India, GC Chambers — Succession certificate India
  6. Objections window — Any person may file objections to the grant of the certificate. (About 45 days (some guides say 30–45 days))
    Sources: Assetly — How to get a succession certificate in India, GetYellow — How long does it take to get a succession certificate
  7. Hearing — Uncontested: a summary hearing on affidavit evidence, then the grant. Contested: a formal inquiry/trial with evidence and witnesses. (Grant often in months 2–4 if uncontested)
    Sources: GC Chambers — Succession certificate India, LegalSeva — Succession certificate and how to get it
  8. Certificate issued — The court passes an order and issues the succession certificate. (Commonly 3–7 months uncontested (guides differ: 3–6, 4–7, 5–7, up to 12 months); contested cases take 1–3 years)
    Sources: Sudhir Rao — Fast-tracking your succession certificate application, Assetly — How to get a succession certificate in India, EzyLegal — How NRIs can obtain a succession certificate in India, GC Chambers — Succession certificate India

Documents to keep ready

  • Death certificate of the deceased
  • Identity proof of the applicant and all other heirs (Aadhaar / PAN)
  • Address proof of the applicant and all other heirs
  • Schedule of the debts and securities claimed, with values — the court fee is charged on this schedule, not on the whole estate
  • No-objection (NOC) statements / affidavits from the other legal heirs
  • Proof of relationship to the deceased

Sources: RegisterKaro — Succession certificate, InvestorHelpdesk — Succession certificate for shares, BankBazaar — Succession Certificate in India, WillJini — Succession certificate for an NRI, Assetly — How to get a succession certificate in India, Sudhir Rao — Fast-tracking your succession certificate application

Common delays

  • An heir who does not consent — the petition becomes contested and takes much longer.
  • Missing or mismatched names across the death certificate, bank records and IDs — fix name/DOB mismatches first.
  • An incomplete asset list — the certificate covers only the debts and securities listed in the petition.
  • A will surfacing after filing — a succession certificate cannot be granted where a valid will exists (Section 370).
