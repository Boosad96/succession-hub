Which certificate do you need?

No will + bank deposits, shares, PF or insurance? Usually a succession certificate from the District Court (Section 372, Indian Succession Act, 1925). Will left behind? Then it is probate (or letters of administration) — a succession certificate cannot be granted. Government pension, gratuity or dues? Usually a legal-heir certificate from the Tahsildar — in the states that issue one. Immovable property? A succession certificate does not transfer title; you need mutation (and probate, if there is a will).

Frequently asked questions

What is a succession certificate?
A court order under Sections 370–390 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, that authorises the legal heirs to collect the deceased person's debts and securities — bank deposits, shares, PF, insurance. It is granted by the District Court where the deceased ordinarily resided. It does not by itself transfer title to immovable property.
Succession certificate vs legal-heir certificate — what is the difference?
A succession certificate is a court order for debts and securities. A legal-heir certificate is issued by the Tahsildar / Revenue Department and identifies who the legal heirs are — used for government pension, gratuity and dues. Not every state issues a legal-heir certificate.
Is there a will? Then what?
If the deceased left a valid will, a succession certificate cannot be granted (Section 370). The route is probate of the will (or letters of administration with the will annexed, if no executor was named).
How long does a succession certificate take?
Guides say 3–6 months for an uncontested case, some say 5–7 months, including a public-notice period of about 45 days for objections. If anyone objects, it becomes a contested case and takes longer.
How much is the court fee?
It varies by state — ad valorem (a percentage of the value) in some states, fixed in others. Use the court-fee estimator; every figure is labelled with its verification status, and the District Court's own figure is final.
