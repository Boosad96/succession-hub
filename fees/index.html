Court-fee estimator — succession certificate

Pick your state and the value of the debts and securities, and get an estimated court fee. Every figure is an estimate. Court fees are fixed by each State Court Fees Act and vary widely — ad valorem (a percentage) in some states, fixed in others — and the District Court's own figure is final. Rows marked “single source” rest on one published source only; rows marked “unverified” are not shown as numbers at all.

Not legal advice. This is a free reference tool built from published sources. Every fee, rule and form shown as verified was checked against at least two independent published sources, and each row links to its sources so you can check it yourself. Court fees, forms and procedures vary by state and by district court practice — always confirm the fee and the format with the District Court or the state portal before filing. If a row is marked “single source” or “unverified”, do not rely on it for a filing. A succession certificate does not by itself transfer title to immovable property. For any filing, a lawyer's review is worth it.
