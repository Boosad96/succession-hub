Legal Heir Certificate in Karnataka — Restricted Issue, Seva Sindhu & the Succession Route

Karnataka is the restricted case: the Bengaluru Urban district page says a legal-heir certificate is now only issued to the kin of deceased government servants. Everyone else has to obtain a succession certificate through their jurisdictional civil court.

Issuing authorityTahsildar (restricted — see note)
PortalSeva Sindhu
FeeNot verified — confirm on the portal
Timeline~15 days (restricted cases)

Who can get one

Only the kin of a deceased government servant, for the deceased's service benefits. The portal is Seva Sindhu, and the timeline for these restricted cases is about 15 days (Seva Sindhu guides say 15–21 working days).

Everyone else

If the deceased was not a government servant, do not apply for a legal-heir certificate — apply for a succession certificate from the District Court instead. Nadakacheri covers family-tree and other revenue certificates, not a general legal-heir certificate.

Documents usually asked for

  • Death certificate of the deceased (mandatory)
  • Identity proof of the applicant and all heirs
  • Address proof
  • Ration card / family register showing all family members
  • Proof of relationship to the deceased
  • Affidavit / joint affidavit from all heirs (where the state asks for one)

Frequently asked questions

Can anyone get a legal-heir certificate in Karnataka?
No. It is now only issued to the kin of deceased government servants, for service benefits.
What should everyone else do?
Obtain a succession certificate through the jurisdictional civil court.
Which portal?
Seva Sindhu (sevasindhu.karnataka.gov.in) for the restricted cases; Nadakacheri for family-tree / revenue certificates.
